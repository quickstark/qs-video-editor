# Documentary Style

Use real recorded material as evidence and let the source determine pacing. Preserve complete thoughts, context, natural sound, and truthful chronology; compression must not manufacture a quotation, reaction, or causal relationship.

Keep graphics sparse and functional: verified titles, identifiers, maps, documents, and simple explanatory diagrams. HyperFrames is primary and Remotion is an optional local fallback. Keep every graphic on a separate editable track and use the timeline's exact rational frame rate.

Start color work neutral and camera-matched. Protect skin tones and highlights. Keep dialogue intelligible, preserve room tone, and deliver dialogue, ambience, music, and SFX separately. Generated assets are disabled until an item-level approval and may never substitute for documentary evidence.

Before editing, confirm intended audience, runtime, chronology rules, exclusions, identity spellings, source consent, archival requirements, music licensing, captions, and any non-literal montage sections.
